An equity flash crash is a sudden, extreme drop in stock prices over a matter of minutes or seconds, followed by a rapid recovery. Many of them are due to errors in automatic trading systems and deliberate market manipulation. 

Each AI-enabled system recommends how its institution should change its equity exposure following the flash crash. 

Give several AI-enabled decision systems the same equity-market shock.  

Give them the same information and the same decision objective.  

Collect their recommendations.  

Measure how similar those recommendations are.  

Then assess what their combined recommended actions could do to the stressed market. 

So, the case becomes: 

Following an equity flash crash, independently operated AI-enabled systems advise different financial institutions on how to adjust their equity exposure. The supervisory agent assesses whether those recommendations converge and how their collective implementation affects the market over successive rounds. 

 

Two comparisons in the demo: 

Primary test: different models from different providers receive exactly the same shock.  

Additional test: several instances of the same model receive the shock, showing the potential effect of widespread reliance on one provider.  

First: assess convergence and market impact.  

Optionally: rerun the same case with one hypothetical safeguard and compare the results. 

Correlated positions could arise from common vendor models, open-source models, datasets, or similar designs. 

The agent should be allowed to propose and conduct follow-up tests when it finds an interesting or uncertain pattern, subject to the regulator’s approval.

The regulator provides and approves the market shock, assessment question, decision systems, evidence, assumptions and boundaries: All of this will be provided by Noha/Theodore 

The agent applies the taxonomy and builds a structured assessment case containing the shock, system profiles, possible transmission pathways and competing explanations: Aryan 

The agent constructs the controlled comparisons required to assess convergence, ensuring that only the intended factor changes between them. The regulator approves and locks the assessment: Aryan/Noha 

The agent presents the approved situation to the AI-enabled decision systems and records their responses: Aryan 

Institutional rules and limits are applied to those responses. A separate quantitative component calculates the resulting effects on prices, liquidity and volatility across successive stages of the stress event: Noha/Theodore 

The agent compares the results and assesses whether observed similarities arise from the common shock, institutional constraints, shared data, similar system designs or another common dependency. Aryan 

The agent compares approved safeguards to determine whether they reduce convergence or market amplification. Aryan 

a “safeguard” here means a hypothetical control tested inside the sandbox. It is a counterfactual question: “If institutions had this control in place, would the same AI recommendations still amplify the shock to the same extent?” 

“what-if” conditions that can be tested after the initial stress assessment. 

An independent verification step checks the evidence, controlled comparisons, calculations and claims. The agent must address failed checks or label unresolved issues. Aryan 

The agent builds the final Convergence Stress Assessment, including findings, uncertainty, alternative explanations, safeguard results, unresolved questions, approvals and the complete audit trail. Aryan 

  

For example: 

Baseline test: the decision systems respond to the flash crash without an additional control.  

Safeguard test: repeat the identical exercise, but assume institutions cannot execute more than 5% of a position immediately.  

Comparison: the agent assesses whether convergence remains and whether the resulting market impact is smaller. 

 

The regulator starts a new case and provides: 

The market shock, such as an equity flash crash.  

The AI-enabled decision systems to assess.  

The market and time period covered.  

Any available evidence, institutional information and assessment boundaries.  

The agent then: 

Structures the shock using the Systemic Stress–Response Taxonomy: initial shock, information available to the systems, possible responses, collective actions, market effects and feedback.  

Organises the decision-system profiles and identifies what information is common to all systems and what is specific to each one.  

Separates confirmed information, regulator assumptions and missing information.  

Retrieves any authorised evidence needed to complete the case, such as market data and authoritative publications.  

Proposes the assessment question and the controlled comparisons required.  

Selects the relevant measures from the pre-approved methodology that you and Theodore will define. The agent does not invent new success metrics for each case.  

Defines what must remain fixed throughout the assessment so that the systems can be compared fairly.  

Produces an assessment plan showing the proposed runs, evidence, assumptions, checks and expected outputs. 

 

Human-in-the-loop: The regulator can approve the plan as proposed, modify it or ask the agent to revise it. The agent cannot begin the assessment, introduce an unstated assumption or materially change the plan without approval.
